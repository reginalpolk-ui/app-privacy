Get Clean Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-06 (draft)

The short version: Get Clean has no accounts and no sign-up. We collect nothing, we track nothing, and there is no server holding anything about you. The few things the app remembers — numbers you save, your state, your checklist progress — live only on your own device, and the app's Erase button removes them permanently.

Who we are

Get Clean is published by Reginal Polk Technologies, Inc., an Illinois corporation based in Bellwood, Illinois. Questions about this policy: reginalpolk@luvvets.com.

What the app stores — on your device only

  • Saved numbers — helplines and state lines you tap "Save" on.
  • Your state — the state you pick in the directory, so it stays set.
  • Checklist progress — the first-step boxes you check off.

These are kept in your browser's or the app's local storage on that device, under keys beginning getclean. They are never transmitted to us or to anyone else. We could not read them if we wanted to — there is no channel for them to reach us.

What we never collect

  • No name, email, phone number, or account of any kind.
  • No location. The app does not ask where you are; you pick your state yourself.
  • No usage analytics, no advertising identifiers, no trackers, no third-party SDKs.
  • No information about what you search, read, or save.

Calls and texts

When you tap a call or text button, your device's phone or messaging app opens with the number filled in. That call or text is between you and the helpline you chose, under that organization's own practices. SAMHSA's National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) is free and confidential, and you never have to give your name.

The read-aloud feature

The overdose steps can be read aloud using your device's own built-in speech feature. The words are the app's published steps — nothing about you is sent anywhere for this to work.

Links that leave the app

A few buttons open public websites — FindTreatment.gov (SAMHSA), the CDC's find-services guide, and NEXT Distro. Those sites are run by their own organizations under their own privacy policies. We do not control them and we do not see what you do there.

Emergencies

Get Clean is an information and routing tool. It does not diagnose, and nothing in it is medical advice; the overdose steps reproduce SAMHSA's published guidance. In an emergency, call 911 first.

Changes

If a future version of Get Clean changes any of this — for example by adding accounts or sync — this policy will be rewritten and re-dated before that version is released, and the change will be described in the app.
